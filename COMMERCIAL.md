# Commercial licence

docier is dual-licensed. If the AGPL does not fit your use — because your
product is proprietary, or because you run it as part of a network service whose
source you do not want to publish — a commercial licence is available.

## When you need one

You need a commercial licence if you:

- ship docier inside a closed-source application;
- run docier as part of a hosted or SaaS product without publishing the
  Corresponding Source of the covered work;
- want to distribute docier as part of a proprietary SDK or platform;
- need terms the AGPL cannot give you, such as an explicit warranty,
  indemnification, or a support commitment.

You do **not** need one to use docier in an open-source project under the AGPL,
to evaluate it, or to use it in development and testing.

## What a commercial licence gives you

- Use in proprietary and closed network products, free of the AGPL's copyleft
  obligations.
- The right to keep your modifications private.
- Terms in writing rather than implied, including any warranty and
  indemnification you require.
- Optionally, support and a security-contact commitment.

## How to obtain one

Contact the maintainer. Include what you are building, how docier would be
distributed (bundled, hosted, or both), and roughly how many end users or
deployments are involved — that is what the terms are usually sized against.

## A note on the AGPL, so the choice is informed

The AGPL is not a trap and it is not vague. It applies when users interact with
the software over a network: at that point you must offer them the source of the
covered work. It does not reach into unrelated software that merely sits beside
docier, and it does not oblige you to publish anything if you are not
distributing or serving the software at all.

The dual licence exists because that obligation is fine for some projects and
unworkable for others, and both are legitimate.
